# *Justice* — Theme Map

Use this as an index for retrieval and discussion. Do not turn it into a checklist the user must cover.

## Welfare and aggregation

**Tension:** If more total happiness is better, what prevents individuals from being treated as containers of utility?

Pressure tests:
- identifiable victim vs statistical benefit;
- intense harm to one vs small benefit to many;
- preferences that are cruel, adaptive, or manipulated;
- whether some goods should not be commensurable.

## Rights and self-ownership

**Tension:** If I own myself, what follows about taxation, labor, bodily autonomy, sale, and coercion?

Pressure tests:
- voluntary but desperate choices;
- selling body parts or services;
- redistribution;
- paternalistic laws;
- whether “ownership” captures our moral relation to our own bodies.

## Consent

**Tension:** Consent appears central to freedom, but consent can be shaped by inequality, necessity, ignorance, and background rules.

Ask:
- Is consent sufficient, necessary, both, or neither for legitimacy?
- What makes consent meaningful rather than nominal?
- Can a fair agreement produce an unjust institution?

## Markets and valuation

**Tension:** A market does more than allocate; it may express or reshape how a good is valued.

Ask:
- What changes when a civic duty, intimate relation, or bodily service acquires a price?
- Is the objection coercion, inequality, corruption of the good, or symbolism?
- Would equal bargaining power eliminate the objection?

## Motive, autonomy, dignity

**Tension:** Doing the outwardly correct act may not be the same as acting for the right reason.

Ask:
- Does morality require freedom from inclination, or can affection and desire coexist with moral worth?
- What does it mean to treat a person as an end rather than merely as a means?

## Fairness, equality, and luck

**Tension:** Outcomes reflect both choice and morally arbitrary circumstances.

Ask:
- Which inequalities require justification?
- If natural talents are unearned, can rewards based on them be deserved?
- Does correcting luck undermine responsibility?

## Merit and desert

**Tension:** We often say winners deserve rewards, but what counts as merit depends on what an institution is for.

Ask:
- Does a university reward virtue, achievement, potential, contribution, or institutional mission?
- Can someone be qualified yet not entitled to the scarce place?
- What is the difference between deserving praise and having a right to a reward?

## Telos and virtue

**Tension:** Aristotle's approach asks about the purpose of a social practice, but pluralistic societies disagree about purposes.

Ask:
- Who decides what an institution is for?
- Can justice avoid arguments about purpose?
- When does “proper purpose” become exclusionary?

## Loyalty, solidarity, and unchosen obligation

**Tension:** Liberal theories often privilege chosen obligations, while ordinary moral life contains family, historical, civic, and communal duties we did not explicitly choose.

Ask:
- Can inherited responsibility be real without inherited guilt?
- Do compatriots have stronger claims on us than strangers?
- What is the moral difference between membership and favoritism?

## Common good and public reason

**Tension:** Political neutrality can protect pluralism, but avoiding moral argument may simply hide which values already govern institutions.

Ask:
- Which moral disagreements can politics bracket, and which must it confront?
- Can civic virtue be cultivated without coercive moralism?
