# Work Pack — *Justice: What's the Right Thing to Do?*

## Identity

- **Work:** *Justice: What's the Right Thing to Do?*
- **Author:** Michael J. Sandel
- **Type:** Non-fiction / moral and political philosophy
- **Pilot use:** A first work for Betaal because the book itself uses cases, intuitions, objections, and competing moral frameworks.

## Purpose of this pack

This is a **discussion scaffold**, not a substitute for the book. It maps arguments, themes, and dilemmas so Betaal can retrieve the right intellectual pressure point.

No copyrighted book text is bundled here. For exact quotations, page-level claims, or close reading, attach a lawful copy, excerpts, notes, or highlights to the runtime.

## Rough argument map

Edition headings can vary, but the book broadly travels through:

1. **Doing the Right Thing** — introduces disputes about welfare, freedom, virtue, and what justice asks of us.
2. **Utilitarianism** — whether maximizing aggregate welfare can justify sacrificing individuals or pricing values that seem resistant to calculation.
3. **Libertarianism / self-ownership** — whether individual liberty and ownership of oneself constrain redistribution and paternalism.
4. **Markets and morals** — whether voluntary exchange settles moral questions; military service and pregnancy/surrogacy are important tests.
5. **Kant** — motive, autonomy, dignity, and why morality may require more than satisfying preferences.
6. **Rawls** — fairness, the original position, arbitrary fortune, equality, and the moral status of distributive outcomes.
7. **Affirmative action** — competing understandings of merit, desert, social purpose, and rights.
8. **Aristotle** — justice, purpose/telos, merit, virtue, and the relationship between institutions and their ends.
9. **Loyalty and membership** — whether obligations can arise from history, community, solidarity, family, or national membership rather than consent alone.
10. **Justice and the common good** — Sandel's case for moral and civic reasoning that cannot be entirely neutral about the good life.

## Discussion stance

Betaal knows Sandel's arguments but **is not Sandel**. It may defend an opposing framework in order to test the user's view, and it should distinguish:

- Sandel's presentation of a view;
- Sandel's criticism of that view;
- Betaal's temporary argumentative role;
- the user's own developing position.

## High-value recurring questions

- When is aggregation morally legitimate, and when does it erase persons?
- How much moral work can consent do?
- Is self-ownership a fact, a moral principle, or a metaphor?
- Are market outcomes morally neutral if participation is voluntary?
- What makes an action morally worthy: consequences, motive, rule, virtue, or something else?
- Can inequalities be deserved if the traits that generate them are morally arbitrary?
- Does merit depend on an institution's purpose?
- Can we owe something we never chose to owe?
- Can a political community remain neutral among conceptions of the good life?
