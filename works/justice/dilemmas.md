# *Justice* — Dilemma Deck

These are conversational starting points. Exact versions and placement should be checked against the user's source when close textual fidelity matters.

## 1. Runaway trolley

**Core pressure:** aggregate welfare vs the moral status of an individual.

Betaal moves:
- “You permitted one death to prevent five. Which feature made that permissible: numbers, indirectness, intent, or something else?”
- Change whether the one is known to the user.
- Change the ratio from 1:5 to 1:1,000,000.
- Ask whether the principle belongs in personal morality, public policy, or both.

## 2. Pushing a person to stop the trolley

**Core pressure:** whether using someone as the means changes the moral structure even when the numbers are identical.

Betaal moves:
- Compare intention vs foresight.
- Ask whether physical contact matters morally or psychologically.
- Ask which theory best explains the user's changed intuition.

## 3. Lifeboat / survival cases

**Core pressure:** whether extreme necessity alters ordinary constraints on harming another person.

Betaal moves:
- distinguish necessity from utility;
- distinguish lottery, consent, vulnerability, and after-the-fact justification;
- ask whether law and morality should give the same answer.

## 4. Price gouging after disaster

**Core pressure:** freedom of exchange vs exploitation, welfare, civic virtue, and fairness.

Betaal moves:
- If high prices increase supply, does that settle the case?
- Would the answer change if everyone had equal wealth?
- Is the objection primarily about outcomes or about what citizens owe one another during emergency?

## 5. Military service: draft, volunteers, paid substitutes

**Core pressure:** coercion, markets, civic obligation, and inequality.

Betaal moves:
- If a volunteer army is consensual, can economic pressure make it morally comparable to a draft?
- Is military service a job, a civic duty, or both?
- If a duty may be bought and sold, what does that say about the duty?

## 6. Surrogacy / contract pregnancy

**Core pressure:** consent, commodification, unequal bargaining power, and what kinds of goods can appropriately be contracted.

Betaal moves:
- If informed consent and equal bargaining power were guaranteed, would every objection disappear?
- Is the concern exploitation, the meaning of parenthood/pregnancy, enforceability, or something else?

## 7. Taxation and redistribution

**Core pressure:** self-ownership and property rights vs social cooperation and distributive justice.

Betaal moves:
- Which part of income is morally prior to the institutions that make earning possible?
- If talent is unearned, what follows—and what does not follow?
- When does taxation become coercion in the morally relevant sense?

## 8. Rawls's original position

**Core pressure:** what principles would be chosen without knowing one's place in society.

Betaal moves:
- Ask whether risk aversion is being smuggled into the thought experiment.
- Ask what information about psychology/culture must remain known.
- Compare fair procedure with fair outcome.

## 9. Affirmative action

**Core pressure:** rights, merit, social purpose, historical remedy, diversity, and what admissions are for.

Betaal moves:
- Separate “I deserved admission” from “I was highly qualified.”
- Ask whether the institution's mission changes what counts as merit.
- Test whether the user's principle generalizes to other selection systems.

## 10. Unchosen obligations and historical wrongs

**Core pressure:** whether responsibility can arise from membership, history, solidarity, or inheritance without individual consent.

Betaal moves:
- Separate guilt from responsibility.
- Compare family obligation with national obligation.
- Ask what makes a group persist morally through time.
